# Injection-head improvement experiments

All variants retain the 42,138,641-parameter encoder and other heads. They are isolated local candidates under `models/candidates/`; the released v2 checkpoint and default policy remain unchanged. `scripts/train_injection_candidate.py` trains only the injection linear-head row from the pinned MIT BPI-Guard source. `scripts/train_injection_joint.py` adds the original synthetic training split. `scripts/train_injection_agentic.py` further adds Enes Deniz's CC-BY-4.0 paired agentic corpus, with attribution. Development-only selection chose C=0.1, threshold 0.55 for BPI-only; C=1.0, threshold 0.50 for joint; and C=1.0, agentic weight 3, threshold 0.65 for the agentic head. Temperatures were fitted on development data. A simple weight blend failed its two-development-set false-positive gates and was rejected (`reports/injection_blend_selection.json`).

The BPI source labels a broader adversarial class than strict prompt injection. The test is synthetic/transformed and can retain source-family similarities after the 12 direct/original-text overlaps removed here. The original synthetic validation is English-only; its perfect injection F1 is a poor guide to the mixed-only test. The joint candidate was chosen without using BPI, synthetic, AgentInjectionBench, zachz or PolyGuardBench test scores in its parameter or threshold grid. BPI, synthetic, AgentInjectionBench and zachz scores had already been inspected during earlier experiments, so the first PolyGuardBench check was a new source for the frozen BPI/joint candidates. The agentic candidate was trained later, after PolyGuardBench was inspected; only Agentic 5K test and PromptWall were newly unopened for that candidate. Source test scores never entered its parameter/threshold grid.

The high AgentInjectionBench F1 is **not** an acceptable outcome: every candidate flags 80% of its benign tool returns, and the benchmark is attack-heavy. PolyGuardBench's attack and benign examples come from different axes, so its combined F1 is also not a matched operational estimate. Its Turkish attack recall rose .516→.978 and English .608→1.000 for the joint candidate, but benign FPR remains .111 Turkish / .194 English. The agentic candidate was chosen on separate paired train/validation splits and improved the untouched Agentic 5K test F1 .628→.872 while cutting FPR .864→.211. That 21% false alarm remains high; PromptWall recall regressed .723→.698, and the earlier AgentInjectionBench benign FPR stayed .800. Neither specialist nor general candidate is promoted. No claim of overall model improvement or production calibration is made.

The first BPI-only candidate also scored .649→.873 F1 against a separate MIT prompt-injection benchmark: 299 rows after four exact-training-overlap exclusions (`reports/injection_candidate_cross_source.json`). That set was viewed before joint training and is not fresh confirmation for later models. Raw counts, revisions, SHA-256 hashes, per-language PolyGuardBench results, calibration, and false-negative rates are in `reports/injection_candidate_bpi.json`, `reports/injection_candidate_holdout.json`, `reports/injection_joint_selection.json`, `reports/injection_agentic_selection.json`, and `reports/injection_agentic_holdout.json`. Source datasets remain in their own repositories; this project commits aggregate metrics only.

The later NeurAlchemy core test was opened after the agentic candidate was frozen. It has 552 broad attack/jailbreak and 390 benign examples, with zero normalized exact overlaps against the BPI, agentic and original synthetic train/development text. F1 improved 0.632→0.805 and recall 0.498→0.726 without changing benign FPR 0.110. Shared semantic templates or upstream-source overlap remain possible; this result is for a broader label than strict tool-output injection. The separate Bordair live-game diagnostic sampled 855 recorded human-player bypass strings and found a lower candidate detection fraction, 0.364 versus v2 0.713. Bordair has no benign controls and labels attacker intent, so the fraction is not recall. Both reports are in `reports/neuralchemy_cross_source.json` and `reports/live_redteam_diagnostic.json`.

The NeurAlchemy source breakdown is mixed: 200 NeurAlchemy-origin positives had false-negative rate 0.730→0.180, but 16 HarmBench positives worsened 0.438→0.938 and 13 WildGuard/JudgeComparison positives worsened 0.077→0.385. These two small subsets have broader safety labels and cannot be interpreted as strict injection miss rates. Their confusion counts and benign false alarms are recorded, rather than hidden by the overall F1.

## Agentic candidate export and edge check

The agentic candidate also has a separate FP32/INT8 ONNX export under `models/exported/injection_agentic/`. The FP32 safetensors and ONNX files are 160.8 and 159.9 MiB; experimental INT8 ONNX is 40.5 MiB. On 256 original synthetic rows, FP32 ONNX matched PyTorch macro F1 (0.6250) with maximum risk-logit difference 0.0000693. INT8 macro F1 was 0.6209 and changed 1.56% of final policy actions versus FP32 on this sample. Batch-one Mac warm p50 in one run was 19.205 ms PyTorch CPU, 13.899 ms MPS, 6.345 ms FP32 ONNX CPU and 10.239 ms INT8 ONNX CPU. These latencies are run-specific; sequential RSS deltas are approximate. Neither INT8 action drift nor model quality is approved for automatic deployment. Exact sizes, parity and timing are in `reports/model_sizes_injection_agentic.json`, `reports/onnx_injection_agentic_parity.json` and `reports/benchmark_injection_agentic.json`.

The opt-in candidate files are versioned in `models/agentic_candidate_manifest.json`; `python scripts/download_artifacts.py --agentic-candidate` checks every downloaded SHA-256. Its release is marked experimental and does not replace v1 or v2. Train data include MIT BPI-Guard, CC-BY-4.0 Agentic Prompt-Injection 5K (Enes Deniz), and the project's synthetic anchors. Policy threshold 0.65 is aligned with the development-selected decision threshold in this candidate directory; this does not validate the final action on operational traffic.

Next experiment: build a family-separated, independently adjudicated mixed-language and tool-output development set with matched benign controls. Refit a head against those domains, then evaluate once on new untouched sources. Keep `untrusted_tool_output` + privileged agent traffic behind the deterministic REVIEW gate until false negatives and false alarms both meet a defined operational target.
